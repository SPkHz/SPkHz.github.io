## More professional colors

### Light mode: replace the bright magenta/purple

That purple reads as neon/playful. These are safer “professional” accents (with good contrast on white):

**Option A (classic / conservative): Deep Navy**

* Accent: **#1F4E79**
* Hover: **#163A5D**
* Muted accent/background tint: **#EAF2FA**

**Option B (modern but still professional): Deep Teal**

* Accent: **#0F766E**
* Hover: **#0B5F59**
* Tint: **#E6F6F4**

**Option C (clean “engineering” vibe): Steel Blue**

* Accent: **#2F5D8A**
* Hover: **#24496C**
* Tint: **#EAF1F7**

If you *want* purple but more professional: go **darker and less saturated** (plum/eggplant), not magenta.

---

### Dark mode: replace the bright cyan/blue

Your dark-mode blue is very bright and “techy,” but it can look a bit arcade/neon. More professional alternatives:

**Option 1 (keep it cool, less neon): Muted Aqua**

* Accent: **#2DD4BF** (aqua/teal)
* Hover: **#22B8A6**
* Subtle tint chips/borders: **rgba(45, 212, 191, 0.12)**

**Option 2 (warmer, very readable on dark): Soft Amber**

* Accent: **#FBBF24**
* Hover: **#F59E0B**
* Tint: **rgba(251, 191, 36, 0.12)**

**Option 3 (calm, “serious”): Soft Sage/Green**

* Accent: **#34D399**
* Hover: **#22C55E**
* Tint: **rgba(52, 211, 153, 0.12)**

### One strong recommendation

Don’t use **purple in light mode** and **cyan in dark mode** as two different “brand identities.” Pick **one accent hue** (navy or teal are safest), then use:

* a **darker** shade in light mode, and
* a **lighter** shade in dark mode.

It instantly looks more cohesive.
