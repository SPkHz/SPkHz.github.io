I’ve added **EE-301 Signals and Systems — Project 01** to my coursework portfolio: **Fourier Transform Applications for Signal Analysis in Electrical Engineering**.

This report treats the Fourier Transform as an engineering instrument rather than a purely mathematical object—using frequency-domain structure to make real signals measurable, filterable, and interpretable.

---

## Topics Covered

- **Signal analysis (Audio + ECG):** spectral content, interference separation, and frequency-domain filtering with time-domain reconstruction  
- **Image processing:** frequency-domain representations, DCT context, and MRI reconstruction framing  
- **Software-defined radio:** spectrum + waterfall interpretation and LTE-style spectral structure  
- **Mechanical diagnostics:** FFT-based vibration analysis for resonance/forcing identification

---

## Importance

Across all four of the aforementioned domains, the Fourier Transform shows up as the same core move:  
**turn time-domain complexity into frequency-domain structure** so you can see what’s dominating, what’s noise, and what you can safely remove or model.

If you’re building filters, analyzing bandwidth, or trying to diagnose a system from what it emits, you’re doing Fourier work—whether you call it that or not.
