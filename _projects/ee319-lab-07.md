## Overview

This lab focused on **measuring**:

- **Differential-mode gain** $A_d = \dfrac{v_o}{v_{id}}$
- **Common-mode gain** $A_{cm} = \dfrac{v_o}{v_{icm}}$
- **Common-mode rejection ratio (CMRR)**

for two classic op-amp measurement front ends:

1. A **single-op-amp difference amplifier** (1× LM741)
2. A **three-op-amp instrumentation amplifier** (3× LM741)

Both circuits were powered from **$\pm 12\,\text{V}$ rails** and intentionally used **5% resistors** to highlight how resistor mismatch limits common-mode rejection.

---

## Key definitions

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/ee319/lab-07/concept_vid_vcm.png" title="Differential vs. common-mode signals and why common-mode gain matters" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}

For two input signals $v_{i1}$ and $v_{i2}$:

\begin{align}
v*{id} &= v*{i2} - v*{i1}\\
v*{icm} &= \frac{v*{i1} + v*{i2}}{2}
\end{align}

A practical “difference” amplifier output can be modeled as:

\begin{align}
v*o &= A_d\,v*{id} + A*{cm}\,v*{icm}
\end{align}

And the common-mode rejection ratio is:

\begin{align}
\text{CMRR} &= \left|\frac{A*d}{A*{cm}}\right| \\
\text{CMRR}_{\text{dB}} &= 20\log_{10}(\text{CMRR})
\end{align}

## Circuit 1 — Single op-amp difference amplifier

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/ee319/lab-07/fig1_difference_amplifier.png" title="Single-op-amp difference amplifier (Figure 1)" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}

### Component values (lab handout)

- Op-amp: **LM741**
- Supplies: **$+V_{CC}=+12\,\text{V}$, $-V_{EE}=-12\,\text{V}$**
- Resistors: **$R_1=R_2=R_3=R_4=1\,\text{k}\Omega$**, **5% tolerance**

### Ideal transfer (with general resistor values)

Using the standard difference-amplifier topology, the non-inverting node is a divider:

\begin{align}
v*p &= \left(\frac{R_4}{R_3 + R_4}\right)v*{i2}
\end{align}

With an (ideal) op-amp in linear operation: $v_n\approx v_p$. The inverting side then gives:

\begin{align}
v*o &= \left(1+\frac{R_2}{R_1}\right)v_p - \left(\frac{R_2}{R_1}\right)v*{i1}\\
&= \left(1+\frac{R*2}{R_1}\right)\left(\frac{R_4}{R_3 + R_4}\right)v*{i2} - \left(\frac{R*2}{R_1}\right)v*{i1}
\end{align}

If the resistor ratios are matched so that:

\begin{align}
\frac{R_2}{R_1} = \frac{R_4}{R_3}
\end{align}

then the common-mode term cancels ideally ($A_{cm}=0$) and:

\begin{align}
A_d = \frac{R_2}{R_1}
\end{align}

### Measurement approach

**Goal:** measure low-frequency, small-signal $A_d$, $A_{cm}$, and then compute CMRR.

Recommended procedure (bench or simulation):

- **Differential-mode test:** drive equal-and-opposite inputs
  - $v_{i1} = -\dfrac{v_{id}}{2}$
  - $v_{i2} = +\dfrac{v_{id}}{2}$
  - Measure $v_o$ amplitude, compute $A_d = v_o/v_{id}$

- **Common-mode test:** drive identical inputs
  - $v_{i1} = v_{i2} = v_{icm}$
  - Measure $v_o$ amplitude, compute $A_{cm} = v_o/v_{icm}$

Notes:

- Keep frequency low (e.g., **100 Hz–1 kHz**) and amplitudes modest (e.g., **50–200 mVpp**) so the LM741 stays in its linear region.
- When computing gain from the oscilloscope, use the same type of amplitude consistently (**Vpp**, **Vrms**, or **Vpk**).

### Worst-case resistor tolerance (why 5% matters)

Even with an ideal op-amp, resistor mismatch produces nonzero $A_{cm}$. A worst-case 5% mismatch example is:

- $R_1=R_4=R_{max}=1\,\text{k}\Omega(1+0.05)=1050\,\Omega$
- $R_2=R_3=R_{min}=1\,\text{k}\Omega(1-0.05)=950\,\Omega$

For this symmetric worst-case:

\begin{align}
A*d &\approx 0.952\\
A*{cm} &\approx 0.0952\\
\text{CMRR} &\approx 10\\
\text{CMRR}\_{\text{dB}} &\approx 20\,\text{dB}
\end{align}

## Circuit 2 — Three op-amp instrumentation amplifier

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/ee319/lab-07/fig2_instrumentation_amplifier.png" title="Three-op-amp instrumentation amplifier (Figure 2)" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}

### Component values (lab handout)

- Op-amps: **3× LM741**
- Supplies: **$+V_{CC}=+12\,\text{V}$, $-V_{EE}=-12\,\text{V}$**
- Resistors:
  - $R_1=R_2=R_3=R_4=1\,\text{k}\Omega$
  - $R_5=47\,\text{k}\Omega$
  - $R_6=1\,\text{k}\Omega$ (appears twice in the schematic)
  - $R_7=47\,\text{k}\Omega$

### Ideal differential gain (rule-of-thumb)

This is the classic 3-op-amp instrumentation amplifier structure:

- The **first stage** provides most of the differential gain.
- The **second stage** is a difference amplifier (with gain set by $R_2/R_1$ when the resistor ratios match).

For the common instrumentation-amp form, a useful ideal approximation is:

\begin{align}
A_d \approx \left(1 + \frac{2R}{R_g}\right)\left(\frac{R_2}{R_1}\right)
\end{align}

In this circuit:

- $R \approx 47\,\text{k}\Omega$ (the feedback resistors in the first stage)
- $R_g$ is the effective resistance between the two inverting nodes. With two $R_6=1\,\text{k}\Omega$ resistors in series, $R_g\approx 2\,\text{k}\Omega$.
- $R_2/R_1 = 1$.

So the expected low-frequency differential gain is roughly:

\begin{align}
A_d \approx 1 + \frac{2\cdot 47\,\text{k}\Omega}{2\,\text{k}\Omega} \approx 48
\end{align}

### Why CMRR improves

Compared to the single-op-amp difference amplifier, the instrumentation amplifier:

- Buffers each input (high input impedance)
- Applies most gain to the **difference** before the final subtractor
- Still depends on resistor matching in the subtractor stage, but typically achieves much lower effective $A_{cm}$ in practice

## Discussion prompts

- Compare measured $A_d$ for Circuit 1 vs Circuit 2. Does the instrumentation amplifier achieve the expected gain order of magnitude?
- Compare $A_{cm}$ and CMRR. Which limitation dominates?
  - Resistor ratio mismatch ($R_2/R_1$ vs $R_4/R_3$)
  - LM741 non-idealities (input offset voltage/current, finite open-loop gain, input common-mode range)
- What practical steps most improve CMRR?
  - Using **0.1% (or better) matched resistor networks**
  - Trimming one resistor in the ratio
  - Using a modern instrumentation amplifier IC
