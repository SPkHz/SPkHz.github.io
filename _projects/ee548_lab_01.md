## Overview

This lab characterizes **laser beam quality** using the **beam propagation ratio** (beam quality factor) **M²**, following the measurement conventions of **ISO 11146**. Two fiber-coupled configurations were compared:

- **Setup A (Single-mode behavior):** 1550 nm light launched into a fiber designed to be single-mode at 1550 nm.
- **Setup B (Multimode behavior):** 850 nm light launched into a **polarization-maintaining (PM) fiber** with a cutoff wavelength near **1250 nm**, so 850 nm propagation supports multiple spatial modes.

The core idea: a nearly ideal Gaussian beam has **M² ≈ 1**, while multimode content and spatial distortion increase **M²**, widen the divergence, and distort the beam profile.

> **Key takeaway:** At 1550 nm, the output beam remained near-Gaussian (**M² ~ 1.03–1.04**). At 850 nm, multimode propagation degraded beam quality, producing a strongly **asymmetric** beam with **Mx² ≈ 1.82**.

## Theory

### Gaussian beam propagation

For an ideal Gaussian beam, the beam radius (1/e² intensity radius) varies with propagation distance as:

$$
W(z) = W_0\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{z - z_0}{z_R}\right)^2}
$$

where:

- $W_0$ is the **beam waist radius** (minimum spot size),
- $z_0$ is the **waist location**,
- $z_R$ is the **Rayleigh length**.

### Beam quality factor (M²)

Real beams deviate from the ideal Gaussian. **M²** quantifies this deviation:

- **M² = 1**: ideal TEM\(\_{00}\) Gaussian beam
- **M² > 1**: increased divergence and/or non-Gaussian spatial structure

In practice (per ISO 11146), M² is determined from measured beam diameters through the focus by fitting the propagation trend and extracting waist size and divergence.

## Procedure (ISO 11146-style workflow)

The acquisition sequence followed the ISO 11146 idea of measuring beam diameters through a focus and fitting the propagation trend:

1. **Align** the fiber-coupled laser source on kinematic mounts.
2. **Collimate** the output using a fiber collimator.
3. Capture a **reference** beam image (no focusing lens) to confirm alignment and centering.
4. Install the **focusing lens** (magnetic mount) to create a waist within the translation range.
5. Translate the beam profiler along **z** and record beam diameters at multiple positions spanning the focus and the surrounding Rayleigh region.
6. Use the instrument software fit to estimate **waist diameter**, **waist location**, **Rayleigh length**, **divergence**, and **M²**.

**Beam diameter definition:** Thorlabs “Beam Width Clip (1/e²)” was used for the beam diameter values exported in the CSV files.

## Discussion

### Single-mode vs. multimode propagation

- In **Setup A**, the fiber supports primarily the fundamental spatial mode at 1550 nm, producing a near-Gaussian output with **M² ≈ 1.03–1.04**.
- In **Setup B**, operating far below the PM fiber cutoff (850 nm vs ~1250 nm cutoff) increases the normalized modal parameter and allows **multiple transverse modes** to propagate.

The measured consequence is a higher beam propagation ratio, especially in the **X axis** where **Mx² ≈ 1.82**, along with a larger divergence.

### Why the distortion is asymmetric in Setup B

The strong difference between **Mx²** and **My²** is consistent with a PM fiber’s internal stress structure (stress rods) creating an **anisotropic index profile**. Below cutoff, multiple modes can propagate and couple differently along each principal axis, leading to:

- unequal waist diameters (175 µm vs 133 µm),
- unequal divergences (11.25 mrad vs 8.51 mrad),
- a non-Gaussian / multi-lobed intensity map.

---

## Sources of error

1. **Launch and alignment sensitivity:** small coupling changes can alter mode excitation, especially for the multimode case.
2. **Stage positioning accuracy:** z-axis offsets shift the inferred waist location $z_0$.
3. **Ambient vibration / air currents:** can introduce centroid jitter during acquisition.
4. **Power stability and camera noise:** impacts diameter estimation at low signal levels.
