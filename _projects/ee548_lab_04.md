## Overview

**Course:** EE-548 — Silicon Photonics  
**Lab:** 04 — Thermal Phase-Shifting Mach-Zehnder Interferometers  
**Date:** 2025-11-07

A **thermo-optic Mach-Zehnder interferometer (MZI)** uses a micro-heater on one arm to change the local refractive index via silicon’s thermo-optic coefficient. This creates a **controllable phase shift** between the interferometer arms, which (1) **slides the broadband interference spectrum** measured on an OSA and (2) can be calibrated into an electrical **π-phase-shift power** (**Pπ**) for later use as a switch/modulator.

## Theory summary

### MZI interference and free spectral range

The relative phase between the arms can be written (for vacuum wavelength $\lambda$) as

$$
\Delta\Phi(\lambda) = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} n_{\mathrm{eff}}(\lambda)\,\Delta L.
$$

For a balanced MZI, one output intensity follows a cosine interference form:

$$
I_{\mathrm{out}}(\lambda) = \frac{I_0}{2}\left[1 + \cos\big(\Delta\Phi(\lambda)\big)\right].
$$

With a broadband source, the spectrum shows periodic fringes in wavelength. The **free spectral range (FSR)** is approximately

$$
\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{FSR}} \approx \frac{\lambda^2}{n_g\,\Delta L},
$$

where $n_g$ is the group index and $\Delta L$ is the arm-length (optical path) imbalance.

### Thermo-optic phase shift and fringe translation

Applying heater power changes temperature $\Delta T$, which changes effective index $\Delta n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and therefore phase:

$$
\Delta\Phi_{\mathrm{heater}} \approx \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}n_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\mathrm{d}T}\right)\Delta T\,L_{\mathrm{heater}}.
$$

In a first-order linear thermal model, $\Delta T \propto P$, so $\Delta\Phi \propto P$.

A measured **peak shift** $\Delta\lambda$ maps to phase using the “one FSR equals $2\pi$” relation:

$$
\Delta\Phi \approx 2\pi\,\frac{\Delta\lambda}{\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{FSR}}}.
$$

So, a **π phase shift** corresponds to **half an FSR** of wavelength translation:

$$
\Delta\Phi = \pi \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Delta\lambda = \frac{\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{FSR}}}{2}.
$$

## Results

### 1) Interference spectrum shift

Heating one arm increases refractive index (positive thermo-optic coefficient in silicon), increasing optical path length and shifting fringes toward **longer wavelengths**.

### 2) FSR → path-length imbalance (derived)

Using the measured $\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{FSR}} \approx 4\ \mathrm{nm}$ and a representative silicon waveguide group index near 1550 nm of $n_g \approx 4.2$:

$$
\Delta L \approx \frac{\lambda^2}{n_g\,\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{FSR}}}
\approx \frac{(1550\ \mathrm{nm})^2}{(4.2)(4\ \mathrm{nm})}
\approx 1.43\times10^2\ \mathrm{\mu m}.
$$

So the designed imbalance is on the order of **$\sim 10^2\ \mathrm{\mu m}$**.

### 3) Phase shift vs. power and $P_{\pi}$

A linear fit of extracted phase vs. electrical power gives:

- **Measured:** $P_{\pi} \approx 48\ \mathrm{mW}$
- **Lecture model target:** $P_{\pi} \approx 41.7\ \mathrm{mW}$

From the measured $P_{\pi}$, the phase efficiency is

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}\Phi}{\mathrm{d}P} \approx \frac{\pi}{P_{\pi}} \approx \frac{\pi}{0.048\ \mathrm{W}} \approx 65\ \mathrm{rad/W}.
$$

Equivalently, expressing phase in units of π:

$$
\frac{\Delta\Phi}{\pi} \approx \frac{P}{P_{\pi}}.
$$

## Discussion

- The measured **$P_{\pi} \approx 48\ \mathrm{mW}$** is within about **15%** of the lecture-model target (**41.7 mW**), which is reasonable given uncertainty in thermal coupling efficiency, heater overlap with the optical mode, and temperature-dependent spreading of heat into the cladding/substrate.
- The **FSR stays approximately constant** during heating for small index perturbations; the dominant effect is a **translation** of the spectrum, not a change in fringe spacing.
- In later labs, this static calibration is reused to bias the MZI at quadrature and measure small-signal bandwidth (thermo-optic time constants typically limit modulation to kHz–low-MHz scales).
