## Overview

This lab characterizes an **integrated resistive heater** placed over one arm of a silicon photonic **Mach-Zehnder interferometer**. By heating the waveguide, the **thermo-optic effect** changes the effective refractive index and produces an **optical phase shift**. Two measurements are emphasized:

1. **DC tuning curve:** MZI output power versus heater electrical power (extracting an approximate $P_\pi$).
2. **Small-signal dynamics:** frequency response (gain + phase) of the thermo-optic phase modulator.

### Key results

- **Phase efficiency (DC):** approximately a **$\pi$ phase shift** occurs at **$\sim 50\,\mathrm{mW}$** of heater power.
- **Bandwidth:** the thermo-optic phase shifter acts as a **low-pass** system with **$f_{3\mathrm{dB}} \approx 12\,\mathrm{kHz}$**.
- **Dynamics:** the Bode magnitude approaches an **effective two-pole roll-off** (~ **−40 dB/decade** at high frequency), and the phase lag trends toward **−180°**.

## Background and theory (compact)

Silicon's positive thermo-optic coefficient implies that heating changes the refractive index. A simplified phase-shift model is:

$$
\Delta\Phi \;=\; \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,\Delta n_{\mathrm{eff}}\,L \;\approx\; \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\left(\frac{dn}{dT}\right)L\,\Delta T
$$

For a balanced MZI with a phase shift applied in one arm, the normalized output intensity follows:

$$
I_{\mathrm{out}} \;=\; \frac{I_0}{2}\,[1+\cos(\Delta\Phi)]
$$

The small-signal thermal dynamics often behave like a **multi-time-constant low-pass**. A useful approximation for a two-time-constant system is:

$$
H(j\omega) \;=\; \frac{\Delta\Phi(j\omega)}{\Delta\Phi_{\mathrm{DC}}}
\;\approx\;
\frac{1}{(1+j\omega\tau_1)(1+j\omega\tau_2)}
$$

## Experimental procedure (high level)

- **DC sweep:** apply a heater bias, record electrical power and the MZI output optical power.
- **AC small-signal test:** bias near mid-fringe, add a small sinusoidal modulation, and measure **modulation depth** and **phase lag** versus frequency.

## Discussion and takeaways

- **Thermal tuning is strong but slow.** Thermo-optic shifters provide large phase shifts with simple electrical drive, but the response is limited by heat diffusion.
- **Two time constants are visible.** The combined gain roll-off and increasing phase lag suggest multiple thermal pathways (local heating + broader substrate heat flow).
- **Design trade-off:** improving thermal efficiency (lower $P_\pi$) often increases thermal isolation, which can reduce bandwidth; improving bandwidth generally requires reducing thermal mass and shortening heat paths.
