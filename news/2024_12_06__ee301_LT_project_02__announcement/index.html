I’ve added EE-301 Signals and Systems — Project 02 to my coursework portfolio: Laplace Transform Applications for System Analysis in Electrical Engineering.

This project treats the Laplace Transform as the “systems lens” of engineering math: converting time-domain differential equations into s-domain algebra so you can see poles, dynamics, stability margins, and transient behavior directly—without guessing from waveforms.

What the project covers

  • Neural decoding (BCIs): Laplace-domain framing for mixed neural measurements and decoding workflows
  • DC-DC converter stability: classic control tools (root locus, Bode, Nyquist) derived from pole/zero structure
  • Electromagnetic partial inductance: modeling that preserves causality and propagation for high-speed structures
  • Memristor modeling: nonlinear device dynamics framed for simulation and circuit-level reasoning

Why it matters

Across all four of the above applications, the underlying move is the same:
step into the s-domain to expose system structure that’s hard to see in the time domain—especially when stability and transient response are the real deliverables.

If you’re modeling a system, designing a controller, or trying to predict how dynamics will behave before you build anything, the Laplace transform will be the right tool for the job, more often than not.
